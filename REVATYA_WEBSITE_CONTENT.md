# Revatya Website Content and Information Architecture

**Version:** 1.0\
**Purpose:** Source of truth for website copy, page structure, content
requirements, and messaging.\
**Brand:** Revatya\
**Core ambition:** Develop a space city that enables human habitation,
research, industry, and logistics beyond Earth.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Brand foundation

### Brand name

**Revatya**

Use the brand name exactly as written. Do not invent alternate spellings
or introduce a different company name.

### One-line description

Revatya is developing a vision and long-term pathway toward a space
city: an interconnected orbital environment for human habitation,
research, industry, and transportation.

### Primary positioning

**A city beyond Earth. Infrastructure for humanity's next frontier.**

Revatya's ultimate focus is a space city. Underwater habitat work is a
potential intermediate engineering and testing programme, not the
company's primary identity or end goal.

### Mission

To investigate and develop the concepts, technologies, partnerships, and
infrastructure pathways that could make a productive, expandable human
city in space possible.

### Vision

A future in which orbital infrastructure supports more than individual
missions: it supports an interconnected environment where people can
live, conduct research, manufacture specialised products, transfer
cargo, and enable further exploration.

### Short brand statement

Humanity has learned to reach orbit. The next challenge is building the
infrastructure that allows people and industries to operate there at
greater scale. Revatya is working toward that future.

### Hero headline options

Use Option A as the default unless design testing suggests otherwise.

-   **Option A:** A city beyond Earth.
-   **Option B:** Building the infrastructure of life beyond Earth.
-   **Option C:** The next chapter of human settlement.
-   **Option D:** From orbit to an entirely new kind of city.

### Hero subheading

Revatya is developing a vision for an orbital city designed to connect
human habitation, scientific discovery, advanced industry, and space
logistics.

### Primary call to action

**Explore the vision**

### Secondary call to action

**View the roadmap**

### Partnership call to action

**Build with Revatya**

### Short description for metadata

Revatya is exploring the architecture, technology, economics, and
development pathway for a future space city.

### Tone of voice

-   Ambitious, composed, thoughtful, and technically curious.
-   Clear and direct rather than grandiose.
-   Optimistic without presenting uncertain outcomes as guaranteed.
-   Specific where evidence exists; explicit about unknowns where it
    does not.
-   Avoid hype, empty startup jargon, and claims that Revatya has
    capabilities or facilities it does not yet have.
-   Use "proposed," "envisioned," "potential," "under investigation,"
    and "long-term" when appropriate.
-   Never imply that a conceptual rendering depicts a real, operational
    city.

### Language to avoid

Avoid unsubstantiated phrases such as: - "The world's first space city"
unless a defensible, carefully qualified basis is provided. -
"Revolutionising humanity forever." - "Fully self-sustaining" as an
achieved capability. - "Zero-gravity manufacturing" as a blanket
promise. Prefer "microgravity-enabled manufacturing" and explain that
benefits vary by process. - "Asteroid mining will power the city" as a
certainty. - Any fabricated customer, investor, partner, technical
result, launch date, funding amount, or milestone.

# 4. Vision page

## Hero

**Eyebrow:** OUR VISION\
**Headline:** From reaching orbit to building in orbit.\
**Body:** Revatya's long-term ambition is to develop a space city: an
interconnected environment where human habitation, research, industry,
and transportation can operate together.

## The problem we are exploring

Human activity in space depends on specialised vehicles, facilities,
supply chains, and expensive transport. As orbital activity expands,
shared infrastructure may create new possibilities---but it must justify
its cost, complexity, and risk.

## The central question

What would it take to create an expandable orbital environment that
supports people and productive activity, rather than a single isolated
mission?

## Principles

1.  **Systems before spectacle:** Architecture must follow operational
    requirements, safety, and feasibility.
2.  **Evidence before claims:** Publish what is known, what is
    hypothesised, and what needs validation.
3.  **Modularity and expansion:** Investigate whether infrastructure can
    be developed incrementally.
4.  **Economic purpose:** Connect proposed capabilities to actual users
    and defensible use cases.
5.  **Human-centred design:** Treat health, comfort, privacy, community,
    and long-duration wellbeing as engineering concerns.
6.  **Collaboration:** The challenge crosses disciplines and
    organisations.
7.  **Long-term responsibility:** Consider environmental impacts,
    orbital sustainability, governance, and responsible use of
    resources.

## What success could look like

A future in which a network of orbital facilities supports sustained
human activity, specialised manufacturing, research, logistics, and
further exploration. A city would be an interconnected infrastructure
system, not simply a large structure.

## Closing CTA

**Help investigate what comes next.**\
If you work in aerospace, research, engineering, architecture,
manufacturing, or related fields, we welcome relevant conversations.

# 5. The City page

## Hero

**Eyebrow:** THE CITY CONCEPT\
**Headline:** Designed as a system. Envisioned as a city.\
**Body:** Explore the proposed components of a future orbital city and
the engineering questions that shape them.

**Status label:** Conceptual architecture --- under exploration.

## Introduction

A city in space would need to do more than keep people alive. It would
need reliable infrastructure, transport interfaces, maintenance, energy,
resource management, places to work, and a reason for people and
organisations to use it. Revatya approaches the city as a connected
system whose design must evolve alongside evidence and feasibility
studies.

## City system cards

### Habitation

Design questions include crew and resident capacity, privacy, health,
emergency refuge, community spaces, environmental quality, and
long-duration human factors.

### Orbital port and logistics

Explore docking interfaces, cargo transfer, servicing, storage, traffic
coordination, assembly, and the relationships between the city and
transport providers.

### Research district

Investigate laboratories and specialised environments for physical
sciences, materials research, life sciences, and other experiments that
require access to space.

### Industrial district

Study the facility requirements, supply chains, safety processes, and
commercial case for selected space-based manufacturing activities.

### Energy and thermal management

Evaluate generation, storage, distribution, redundancy, heat rejection,
and the impact of system failure.

### Life-support infrastructure

Consider atmosphere management, water recovery, waste handling, resource
loops, monitoring, maintenance, and backup systems.

### Construction and maintenance

Explore modular assembly, robotic assistance, inspection, repair, and
the feasibility of adding capacity over time.

### Human environments

Consider exercise, recreation, social connection, accessibility,
work-life boundaries, and psychological wellbeing.

## Design philosophy

The city's form should emerge from requirements rather than from visual
spectacle alone. Its layout must respond to transport, safety,
maintenance, energy, thermal management, human factors, and economics.
Visual concepts should clearly be labelled as conceptual until supported
by a developed engineering design.

## Interactive city atlas concept

Allow visitors to select a district and see: - Its intended purpose -
Potential users - Key supporting systems - Main engineering questions -
Dependencies on other districts - Current evidence/status - Relevant
research articles

# 6. Applications page

## Hero

**Eyebrow:** WHAT A CITY COULD ENABLE\
**Headline:** More than a destination. An environment for activity.\
**Body:** Revatya is investigating the potential roles an orbital city
could play in research, industry, logistics, habitation, and future
exploration.

## Application 1: Microgravity-enabled manufacturing

**Opportunity:** Some manufacturing processes may benefit from
microgravity or the ability to conduct operations in space.

**Questions to investigate:** - Which specific processes have
demonstrated or credible advantages? - Can the benefit be reproduced
consistently? - How will feedstock reach the facility and products
return to customers? - What are the costs of production, transport,
maintenance, and quality assurance? - Is an orbital city necessary, or
would a smaller facility be sufficient?

**Potential users:** Materials researchers, specialist manufacturers,
life-sciences companies, and industrial research organisations.

**Status:** Potential application requiring case-by-case validation.

## Application 2: Orbital port and logistics

**Opportunity:** Shared infrastructure could support cargo transfer,
spacecraft servicing, storage, assembly, resupply, and movement between
orbital destinations.

**Questions to investigate:** - What demand could justify a permanent
facility? - Which orbits and transport interfaces would be useful? -
What services would customers pay for? - How would orbital debris,
safety, traffic coordination, and regulation be handled? - What
infrastructure can be reused or shared?

**Potential users:** Spacecraft operators, logistics providers,
satellite-service companies, research organisations, and future
commercial space operators.

**Status:** Long-term infrastructure thesis requiring demand and
feasibility analysis.

## Application 3: Research and experimentation

**Opportunity:** A dedicated facility could support repeated access to
specialised environments, equipment, and operational support.

**Questions to investigate:** - Which research needs sustained or
frequent access to orbit? - What environmental controls and safety
provisions are necessary? - Which facilities could serve multiple
research customers? - How would experiments, data, samples, and
equipment move through the facility?

**Potential users:** Universities, laboratories, scientific agencies,
and commercial research teams.

## Application 4: Space resource utilisation

**Opportunity:** Prospecting and processing resources beyond Earth could
eventually support selected space activities.

**Questions to investigate:** - Which resources are useful and
accessible? - What prospecting and extraction methods are viable? - What
processing is possible in situ? - What are the transport and energy
requirements? - What demand exists for the resulting materials? - What
legal, environmental, and governance issues apply?

**Status:** Highly uncertain long-term opportunity. Do not describe
asteroid mining as commercially established or inevitable.

## Application 5: Habitation, training, and tourism

**Opportunity:** Future orbital infrastructure may support specialist
personnel, research crews, training, and commercial visitors.

**Questions to investigate:** - What level of safety and reliability is
required? - What services can be provided in orbit? - What are the
transport and operating costs? - What demand could support the
service? - How can facilities support human health and wellbeing?

## Application 6: Assembly and servicing

**Opportunity:** Certain spacecraft or large structures may benefit from
assembly, inspection, repair, or servicing in orbit.

**Questions to investigate:** - Which structures are difficult or
inefficient to launch fully assembled? - What robotics, tools, and crew
capabilities would be needed? - What interfaces and safety standards are
required? - Could servicing reduce lifecycle costs?

## Closing

The existence of a possible application does not establish a business
case. Revatya will need to identify specific customers, validate
technical advantages, estimate total costs, and compare each opportunity
against simpler alternatives.

**CTA:** Explore the space economy

# 7. Space Economy page

## Hero

**Eyebrow:** THE COMMERCIAL THESIS\
**Headline:** A city must create value, not only occupy space.\
**Body:** Revatya is exploring whether shared orbital infrastructure
could make selected space activities more useful, accessible, or
economically viable.

## Why shared infrastructure?

In principle, a shared facility can distribute certain fixed costs
across multiple users and connect services that would otherwise be
developed separately. Whether this creates a real advantage depends on
demand, utilisation, transport costs, operating complexity, and the
alternatives available.

## Business-model hypotheses to investigate

These are hypotheses for analysis, not claims of existing revenue: -
Leasing specialised research or laboratory capacity - Providing cargo
handling, storage, and orbital logistics services - Hosting selected
manufacturing processes - Offering assembly, inspection, or servicing
facilities - Providing habitation and support services for professional
users - Supporting training and carefully regulated commercial visits -
Providing shared utilities or infrastructure to multiple operators

## Validation framework

For each opportunity, document: 1. Customer and unmet need 2. Evidence
of demand 3. Technical requirements 4. Transport and operating costs 5.
Capital requirements 6. Utilisation and pricing assumptions 7.
Regulatory and safety requirements 8. Competing alternatives 9. Key
uncertainties 10. Criteria for proceeding, pausing, or abandoning the
idea

## Closing

The commercial case for a city should be built from validated use cases
and infrastructure economics---not from market-size figures alone.

**CTA:** Discuss a potential commercial or research use case

# 8. Roadmap page

## Hero

**Eyebrow:** THE DEVELOPMENT PATH\
**Headline:** A long-term ambition. A sequence of testable questions.\
**Body:** Revatya's roadmap organises the journey into stages, each
intended to reduce uncertainty and inform the next decision.

**Status legend:** Researching · Proposed · In development · Validated ·
Deferred

Never display a status unless it reflects the actual state of work.

## Stage 01: Foundational research

**Objective:** Establish a rigorous understanding of space habitats,
orbital infrastructure, engineering constraints, commercial activity,
and existing capabilities.

**Workstreams:** - Literature and industry review - System requirements
and constraints - Potential customers and applications - Technology
readiness assessment - Initial economic models - Research and
collaboration priorities

**Exit criteria:** A documented problem map, evidence base, key
uncertainties, and prioritised hypotheses.

## Stage 02: Extreme-environment test programmes

**Objective:** Identify relevant environmental and operational
principles that can be tested safely on Earth.

**Potential focus areas:** - Environmental monitoring - Redundancy and
fault response - Water management - Closed-environment operations -
Human factors - Maintenance procedures - Remote operations

**Boundary:** Underwater conditions differ substantially from space.
Underwater tests cannot validate orbital microgravity, vacuum exposure,
radiation shielding, launch loads, or orbital operations.

**Exit criteria:** A justified test plan, measurable criteria,
documented results, and a clear account of what the tests do and do not
establish.

## Stage 03: Technology and system prototypes

**Objective:** Build or evaluate selected components and prove their
performance against explicit requirements.

**Potential workstreams:** Monitoring systems, autonomous inspection,
selected life-support subsystems, modular interfaces, robotics, or other
topics chosen after research.

**Exit criteria:** Test reports, repeatable measurements, known
limitations, and a decision about the next stage.

## Stage 04: Initial orbital infrastructure concept

**Objective:** Develop a technically and commercially grounded concept
for an initial orbital platform or service.

**Workstreams:** - Mission and orbit selection - Transport and launch
dependencies - Habitat and payload requirements - Power and thermal
architecture - Life-support and safety - Target users and demand -
Operating model and capital requirements - Partner and regulatory
requirements

**Exit criteria:** A defensible feasibility assessment and explicit
go/no-go criteria. This stage does not imply that Revatya has a launch
plan or operational facility.

## Stage 05: Expansion toward a city

**Objective:** Investigate how additional modules and services could
connect into an expandable urban system.

**Workstreams:** - Modular expansion - Shared utilities and
infrastructure - Transport and logistics networks - Human habitation and
community - Industrial and research districts - Governance, safety, and
operations - Long-term economic sustainability

**Exit criteria:** A credible systems architecture and a staged
expansion case supported by evidence.

## Roadmap governance

Each milestone should contain: - Name and objective - Current status -
Owner, if applicable and publicly disclosed - Evidence or supporting
documentation - Dependencies - Risks and unresolved questions -
Completion criteria - Last updated date

**CTA:** Contribute expertise or propose a research collaboration

# 9. Research page

## Hero

**Eyebrow:** RESEARCH AND ENGINEERING\
**Headline:** Start with the hard questions.\
**Body:** Revatya's research focus is to understand the constraints that
shape a viable space city and identify where evidence, design, or

## Research programmes

### Habitat systems and life support

Atmosphere revitalisation, water recovery, waste processing,
environmental monitoring, redundancy, maintainability, and failure
response.

### Radiation and human health

Exposure management, shielding approaches, health monitoring, crew
wellbeing, and the implications of long-duration habitation.

### Orbital architecture and construction

Modular interfaces, assembly, structural design, inspection, robotic
assistance, maintenance, and expansion.

### Power and thermal systems

Power generation, storage, distribution, thermal control, heat
rejection, and resilience.

### Human factors and habitation

Privacy, accessibility, sleep, exercise, community, psychological
wellbeing, emergency procedures, and design for long-duration use.

### Orbital logistics and services

Docking, cargo transfer, storage, servicing, spacecraft interfaces, and
the relationship between infrastructure and transport systems.

### Manufacturing and industrial applications

Specific processes that may benefit from space-based operations,
customer requirements, production quality, transport, and commercial
feasibility.

### Economics, governance, and sustainability

Capital requirements, operating costs, demand, regulation, safety,
orbital debris, responsible resource use, and long-term governance.

## Research status framework

Label content as one of: - **Established background:** Supported by
reliable published evidence. - **Research question:** An unresolved
issue being investigated. - **Revatya hypothesis:** A proposition
requiring validation. - **Concept design:** A proposed arrangement or
architecture. - **Test result:** A result from a documented test with
methodology and limitations. - **Validated requirement:** A requirement
supported by evidence appropriate to its intended use.

1.  Do not invent company history, founders' biographies, staff,
    investors, funding, facilities, partners, customer endorsements,
    press coverage, or completed work.
2.  Do not claim Revatya has built, launched, tested, or validated
    anything unless explicitly confirmed.
3.  Mark concept art and speculative architecture as conceptual.
4.  Distinguish established facts from hypotheses, plans, and
    aspirations.
5.  When technical claims are published, provide reputable references
    and dates where practical.
6.  Do not invent market sizes, market shares, revenue forecasts,
    timelines, or technical performance.
7.  Use conservative, qualified language for asteroid resource
    utilisation, microgravity manufacturing, and commercial space
    tourism.

    transferable principles; it cannot substitute for space-specific
    validation.

    customer counts, or progress statistics.
10. If content is unknown, use a well-designed omission or an explicitly
    marked placeholder rather than fabricated copy.

# 20. Image and video content direction

## General visual direction

Create a coherent family of visual assets. Prefer realistic, monumental
architectural visualisations with consistent materials, lighting, camera
language, and orbital context. Use a mix of: - Interior perspectives at
human eye level - Wide exterior views of the city above Earth - Orbital
port and docking scenes - Research and manufacturing interiors -
Architectural cutaways and sectional diagrams - Clean technical
illustrations

## Essential hero image brief

A vast, inhabited orbital city viewed from inside at human eye level.
The foreground includes refined architectural details and a public
observation area. Through enormous windows, Earth fills part of the
background. Spacecraft approach or dock at distant infrastructure. The
city should feel monumental, interconnected, and designed for long-term
human activity. Photorealistic architectural visualisation, physically
plausible lighting, restrained silver and white materials, sophisticated
proportions, no text, no logos, no watermark, no fake UI.

## Video use

Use video only where it adds meaningful value: - A restrained hero loop
revealing the orbital city and Earth - A slow visual transition from
city exterior to interior - A city-atlas or architecture explainer -
Short clips accompanying real progress updates, if available

Avoid using video on every section. Respect reduced-motion preferences,
provide a static fallback, compress assets, and avoid autoplaying audio.

## Video truthfulness

Generated video is illustrative. Never present it as footage of a real
Revatya facility, actual mission, real spacecraft operation, or
validated design.
